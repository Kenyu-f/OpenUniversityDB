## 1. On-Campus Undergraduate Housing — The House System

- Caltech **guarantees on-campus housing** and **requires undergraduates to live on campus for their first two years**; most students choose to stay on campus for all four years.
- The system is explicitly modeled on the **residential college system of Oxford and Cambridge**, though in scale/character it more closely resembles the house systems at Yale and Harvard.
- **History:** Established in **1931**, when Caltech's existing fraternities were disbanded and recast as the original four Houses — **Blacker, Dabney, Fleming, and Ricketts** — located in what became known as the **South Houses** (the oldest Houses on campus). Growth in the 1960s added the **North Houses** — **Lloyd, Page, and Ruddock** (Ruddock was later renamed **Venerable**). **Avery House** opened in 1996 as a mixed undergraduate/graduate/faculty residence and was formally integrated into the House system for the 2003–2004 school year.
- There are **11 residence halls** in total: **8 traditional Houses** plus **3 unaffiliated (non-House) halls**, including the newer **Bechtel Residence** (Caltech's newest hall, 211 bed spaces, individual rooms and suites, courtyard, and dining hall; each bedroom is just under 100 sq. ft.).
- **South Houses:** the oldest on campus; single, double, and triple-capacity bedrooms (mostly singles); 64 bed spaces; all bathrooms are gender-neutral.
- **Room assignment process:** First-year students are temporarily assigned a room on arrival, go through orientation, and then participate in **Rotation** — a structured process of visiting Houses and meeting current residents before choosing where to live. This "see it before you choose it" approach is distinctive relative to many universities' randomized first-year assignment systems.
- Every undergraduate room includes a desk, chair, drawers, closet space, and an XL twin bed; every residence building requires Caltech ID card access.
- Houses include dining halls, lounges, kitchens, and House-specific common/social spaces, and each House maintains its own traditions and social calendar (study breaks, movie nights, hiking/skiing trips, off-campus excursions).

## 2. On-Campus Graduate Housing

- **Catalina Apartments ("the Cats")** — located at the northwest end of campus, this is the center of Caltech's graduate residential community, housing **up to 450 graduate students** (single and partnered). All units include a private patio/balcony, carpeting, furnished bedroom(s) and living room, central A/C and heat, and a fully equipped kitchen. On-site laundry (card-operated) and recreation centers are included; water and trash service are paid by Caltech.
- **Off-campus Caltech-owned lease properties** — many graduate students live in Caltech-owned properties in close proximity to (but not directly on) campus, ranging from apartments to single-family homes.
- **New graduate student housing application window (2026–27 cycle):** opened **April 15, 2026**, deadline **April 30, 2026**. Newly admitted students must list at least three eligible housing options on their application. After the first year, returning graduate students go through an **annual Housing lottery**.
- **Room configuration options** include: Catalina 2-Bedroom Furnished (private room, shared common space with one roommate), Catalina 4-Bedroom Furnished (private room, shared common space with three roommates), and Off-Campus Studio Furnished (renting an entire studio alone).
- Visiting (non-Caltech) students are **not eligible** for the standard lottery but may be considered for leftover assignments or guest housing.
- **The Athenaeum** (Caltech's faculty club) is open to graduate students for a small monthly membership fee, offering a more formal dining option.
- **Pets are not permitted** in Caltech housing; personal property is not insured by the Institute (renters' insurance is recommended).
- **Guest policy:** graduate residents may host an overnight guest for a maximum of **three nights per month**.

## 4. Off-Campus Housing — Surrounding Neighborhoods

Caltech's campus sits in central Pasadena. Students who move off-campus (mainly graduate students, since undergrads are largely house-system-based) commonly look in the following areas, roughly ordered by proximity/character:

| Neighborhood | Character | Distance/Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Central Pasadena / Old Town Pasadena** | Dense, walkable, restaurants/shops, higher rent | Closest urban core to campus |
| **South Pasadena** | Quieter, residential, tree-lined, well-regarded schools | Adjacent to Caltech, popular with grad students/postdocs with families |
| **San Marino** | Affluent, quiet, residential | Slightly further; higher-end single-family rentals |
| **Sierra Madre** | Quiet, tree-lined streets, small-town feel | A bit further from campus; often cited as a calmer alternative |
| **Altadena** | Residential, more affordable | North of Pasadena; longer commute |
| **Eagle Rock / Highland Park** | More affordable, part of LA's eastside | Further; more commute-dependent |
| **Monrovia / Arcadia / Flintridge / La Cañada / La Crescenta / Montrose** | Suburban, family-oriented | Further out; typically requires a car |

⚠️ These are general reputational characterizations aggregated from off-campus housing platforms, not Caltech-vetted safety ratings. Prospective renters should visit neighborhoods at different times of day before signing a lease, as recommended by multiple off-campus housing guides.

## 6. Transportation Considerations

- Pasadena is served by the **LA Metro L Line (Gold Line)**, which connects Pasadena to downtown Los Angeles — relevant for students living slightly further out (e.g., Eagle Rock, Highland Park) who want to avoid relying entirely on a car.
- Closer neighborhoods (South Pasadena, Central Pasadena, San Marino) are realistically walkable or bikeable to campus; more distant suburbs (La Cañada, Monrovia) generally require a car.
- Caltech's own campus is compact and highly walkable/bikeable, which is one reason on-campus (or near-campus) housing remains popular even among graduate students who could live further out.
